WINNER: Doručenka (evolutionary) — as backbone, with 12 binding amendments absorbed from the three breaker reports and grafts from Ledger and Bill of Lading

RATIONALE:
All three candidates independently converged on the identical skeleton — hub-ordered append-only journal in hub.db, payload-free poke over the already-open Hocuspocus stateless channel (verified present and unused), per-peer cursor + per-file ancestor store (Dropbox Synced Tree), ONE pure three-way decision table, classifier as sole membership oracle, checkout as the cell's only serving truth, R2 demoted to write-behind durability, CRDT doc lanes untouched. That convergence (which also matches the prior-art dossier's universal endgame: Dropbox journal+cursor, CouchDB _changes+checkpoint, Syncthing index+sequence) means the winner is decided by migration quality and factual accuracy, not by architecture. Doručenka wins on both: its breaker found its factual grounding 'unusually accurate — nearly every checkable claim held up'; its Increment 0 (delete dead autocommit.ts, unify the twin cold-start appliers — fixing the LIVE migrate-seed recover-seed-dup fallthrough — add the F6 budget) is a zero-wire-change win that attacks trap #2 first, where Ledger defers applier unification to M6 and Bill of Lading to inc 6; its increments keep every v1 lane literally untouched until the replacement has soaked (cleanest rollback story of the three); and it avoids Bill of Lading's two worst SEV-1s by construction (it HAS a local-state enumerator via the stat-cache boot reconcile, and its push half is ledger-mediated). Bill of Lading's Part I is nonetheless valuable — the serious GitSync development and rejection should be recorded as a DDR so git-as-transport is never re-litigated. Ledger's clean-slate is architecturally equivalent but its migration carries more unforced risk (in-process drain vs the un-root-caused DDR-222 segfault, M6 rollback contradiction, journal-only-truth overreach without a walk reconciler). Breaker findings are absorbed, not hand-waved: the two genuinely load-bearing cross-candidate holes — (1) cells rehydrate from a ≤6h-old backup generation on EVERY wake, so a hub.db-only journal silently rewinds seqs (Doručenka's epoch-survives-restore rule is affirmatively wrong; Ledger/BoL's epoch-per-restore makes full re-anchor + conflict-copy spam a daily event) and (2) the M1/inc-1 'cell child hears the poke on its existing loopback provider' claim is false fleet-wide (CELL_LIVE_PAIRING is a one-tenant pilot, verified) — are resolved as design changes: journal-tail write-behind to R2 per append with replay-before-epoch-decision at rehydrate (epoch rotates only on unreconstructible rewind, making both failure modes rare instead of daily), and a non-pairing-gated ctl-only channel for the hub→child nudge. Every other fixable hole from all three reports is bound into the architecture or phasing below. Net effect: ~2,200–2,900 lines deleted vs ~1,400 added, seven lanes → two, three reconcilers → one primitive, and every Map-C trap gets a structural property rather than vigilance.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
- Deletion propagation default (Increment 6): ship `linkedHub.propagateDeletes` ON with the breakers (completes the iCloud mental model the user asked for) or OFF/opt-in for one extra release (Plane-B v1's conservative posture)? This is a trust trade-off only a human can price after living with the breakers in dogfood.
- Breaker UX: when the mass-delete / tombstone-storm / first-anchor-conflict breakers fire, should sync HOLD until the user answers in the Sync panel (safest, but a headless/unattended desktop stalls indefinitely), or auto-proceed conservatively (quarantine everything, propagate nothing) after a timeout?
- Conflict-copy attribution vs privacy: `.maude-conflict-<ts>-<label>` propagates into the shared tree — should <label> be the hostname (best attribution, mild privacy leak, same class as today's syncMeta.by), a user-chosen device label, or an opaque short id?
- Support window for journal-less self-hosted hubs: how many releases must a new desktop retain the legacy pull/push client after Increment 5? (Cost: dormant code + test matrix; benefit: self-hosters upgrade on their own cadence. Proposal in the plan is ≥2 — confirm or shorten.)
- Doručenka peer-list semantics: after how long is a departed peer auto-labeled stale and excluded from the 'everywhere' aggregate (14 days? 30?), and is manual dismissal allowed to permanently remove a peer from a project's receipt view?
- R2 cost posture: journal tail per append + CAS for all classes + trash-prefixed quarantine copies + never-GC grows per-tenant storage monotonically. Accept as the price of durability, or surface a per-tenant storage figure to the customer (and if so, where in the product)?
- Is the shared-doc epilogue (Increments 7–8) in scope for THIS arc, or a separate follow-up arc? It is separable, ~800 lines of deletion, and erases the residual drift class — but it extends the calendar by ~2 releases and touches the doc plane the rest of the redesign deliberately leaves alone.

Slots into Increment 1 (hub journal). This single graft resolves Doručenka's #1 hole (silent seq rewind), Ledger's own #2 (orphaned browser-only uploads), and BoL's #2 (daily epoch-churn conflict spam) at once.
- From Ledger — crossing-write self-detection: a remote journal row whose hash equals an in-flight outbox entry is treated as self (ancestor adopt, no conflict-aside). Slots into the desktop applier (Increment 3).
- From Ledger — client-side reset/poke cooldown against hostile-hub reconcile spam, and the 2 GiB aggregate per-batch pull budget as the F6 implementation. Increment 3.
- From Ledger — doručenka state granularity (local-only → pushing(%) → on-hub → at-peer → ui-healed → everywhere) merged into Doručenka's four-checkmark model; 'refusal outranks cursor' fix applied (persistent refused-set per peer, cursor alone never renders delivered). Increments 2–3.

- From Ledger breaker — hub-side walk-import reconciler kept PERMANENTLY: periodic + boot checkout walk diffed against journal compaction, appending walk-import rows (reuses file-manifest.mjs machinery; sha cache persisted to hub.db; runs post-bind, serve-stale-until-repaired). Catches git-level restores, missed child write sites, and class flips. Increment 1.

Increment 3, ships WITH the ancestor store, not after.
- From Bill of Lading — CI grep pinning every rename( in a write door to an adjacent journal append (the ledger-vs-checkout drift tripwire). Increment 1.
- From Bill of Lading breaker — conflict-copy naming .maude-conflict-<ts>-<label> (NOT *.sync-conflict-*: ~/git runs real Syncthing and the identical pattern would be unattributable), classifier additionally refuses foreign *.sync-conflict-* files from membership. Increment 3.
